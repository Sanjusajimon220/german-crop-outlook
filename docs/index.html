How accurate is the forecast?

All numbers below come from the test years 2018-2025, which the model never saw during fitting, and from three federal states that were held out completely (Schleswig-Holstein, Baden-Wuerttemberg, Brandenburg). The JRC MARS forecast and the Destatis harvest reporters are compared at the same date: for each of their publications only our forecast from that date or earlier is used. Errors are in % of the average yield (RMSE); lower is better. Test forecasts use data up to each date in near-final versions (hindcast).

Method

Model. A daily crop growth model (light interception, temperature, soil water, nitrogen, heat and wetness effects, breeding progress) runs for every district from the observed sowing date. A learned correction and a data-driven gradient-boosting model refine it; satellite greenness (MODIS NDVI over cropland) enters from 2000. During the season, the weather after the forecast date is replaced by each of 39 past seasons (1979-2017), which gives the forecast distribution.

v10 additions. An annual district level update learned from the model's own past errors (district yields published two years earlier), a national 3-year update for grain maize, and a barley model refitted every 3 years to follow fast breeding progress. Ranges combine weather uncertainty with a shared year-wide error and a local error; for barley and grain maize the error size adapts to the misses of recent years.

v11.1 (10 Oct 2026). Same forecasts as v10. State-level ranges now include a state-wide error part (state coverage on training years 69-84 % instead of 50-72 %). 2026 forecasts exist for every forecast date (frozen for the spring-2027 check). A crop-specific Sentinel-2 correction improved early forecasts by 3-8 % in a 2019-2023 backtest but is not used: a check on 2023 showed its district inputs are dominated by sampling noise (25 fields per district). It returns only after a refit on larger samples.

v12 (11 Oct 2026). Same forecasts again. Barley ranges widened where the training years showed them too narrow (district x 1.19, national x 1.63). The Sentinel-2 correction was also tested on 2024, a year it had not seen, and made district errors worse in all five cases, so it stays out.

Data. DWD HYRAS weather, DWD stations, CM SAF SARAH-3 sunlight, BGR soils, DWD phenology, Destatis district yields (regional database 41241) and Duden et al. 2024, fertiliser statistics, MODIS MOD13Q1, ESA WorldCover. Benchmarks: JRC MARS Bulletins 2009-2026, Destatis Fachserie 3 Reihe 3.2.1 2005-2022.

Validation. Every design choice was made on the training years 1979-2017 by forward validation (fit up to a year, predict the years after), mostly in two separate periods, with the rules written down before results were seen. The test years were looked at once, after freezing the model with file fingerprints. The frozen predictions for 2026 will be checked against the official 2026 district yields in spring 2027.

Known limitations. The model is still somewhat too optimistic in very dry and hot summers, above all for maize. Against MARS the forecast is level in most month-by-crop comparisons, clearly better for wheat in June and clearly worse for barley in August. At state level the Destatis harvest reporters, who observe the crop, are better. Grain maize district data end in 2021. MODIS satellites are retiring; crop-specific Sentinel-2 greenness is being added (next version).

Field level (NRW study areas). Weekly 10 m green LAI and NDVI for every field in four 8 × 8 km areas, seasons 2021–2025: measured from clear Sentinel-2 images (green LAI by a neural network trained on radiative-transfer simulations of cereal canopies; for maize, sugar beet, potato and rapeseed crop-specific networks are being tested), gaps up to 30 days by a straight line, longer gaps by a model driven by the Sentinel-1 radar change, each value with its typical error. Single bad clear images (haze, missed cloud edges) are removed from LAI. Tests and comparisons with Copernicus HR-VPP and LAI 300 m are on the Accuracy page.
